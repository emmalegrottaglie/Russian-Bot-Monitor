# Narrative keywords

Why the tracker has a "narratives" group, where each keyword comes from, and what it can and cannot show.

## Why

The right-wing group follows communities and searches named after parties and figures (r/Conservative, "MAGA", "AfD"). Published research on Russian state-linked influence operations describes something different. They push narratives (Ukraine aid as waste, Zelensky as illegitimate, culture-war topics) and attacks on named people, rarely party names. A group built on party names could miss most of what such operations post. The narratives group follows the talking points instead.

## What this group can and cannot show

- **It measures a topic, not an operation.** A search for "Zelensky" counts every post that uses the name: news, supporters, critics and debunkers. Researchers warn that keyword and hashtag volume cannot separate coordinated accounts from real users.
- **The topics move with the news.** Zelensky and Ukraine aid spike with events in the war, unrelated to bots. The strikes on Yandex were news themselves.
- **Documented reach was small.** ISD calls Operation Overload's influence underwhelming. Even if operations went quiet, their posts may be too few to move these counts.
- **Zelensky has extra weight.** Four of the eight searches are the same name in different languages, so the group median leans towards that one topic.

So this group gets the same tests as every other group: compared with the non-political control group, and with the same measure taken on random pre-strike dates. A drop after the strikes that also shows in the control group, or that random dates often produce, is not evidence. No change in this group would not show that bots are absent either.

## Anti-trans rhetoric: tracked on Twitter only

Doppelganger used anti-trans topics, and NGOs have measured a rise in anti-trans rhetoric on Twitter: CCDH and the Human Rights Campaign (2022), CCDH's "Musk Bump" (2022) and ISD (2025). The About tab cites them. Beth's Anti-Transphobia Library (bethylamine.github.io) catalogues the talking points and code words. We tested whether Bluesky could measure this rhetoric, in October 2026.

**Volume.** Exact daily counts, 1 September to 17 October 2026. Each day was read in one search, which returns every post when there are fewer than 100.

| Search | Pre-strike mean (Sep 15 to Oct 7) | Oct 8 to 11 | Oct 12 to 17 |
|---|---|---|---|
| "women's sports" | 82 | 78 | 65 |
| groomer | 41 | 33 | 41 |
| "protect women" | 35 | 20 | 35 |
| AGP | 24 | 22 | 22 |
| groomers | 21 | 19 | 20 |
| "gender critical" | 20 | 14 | 31 |
| "gender ideology" | 15 | 9 | 13 |
| "trans ideology" | 6 | 3 | 6 |
| "what is a woman" | 6 | 6 | 7 |
| "biological male" | 3 | 5 | 10 |
| "adult human female", autogynephilia | 2 | under 6 | 2 |
| German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese "gender ideology" terms | 0 to 5 | 0 to 7 | 0 to 4 |

On every strike day, each term stayed within the range it had on pre-strike days. "TRAs" and "TERF" are left out: "TRAs" matches the Spanish word "tras", and "TERF" is used mostly by trans people and allies.

**What the posts say.** We hand-checked about 50 recent posts per term, sampled from two points in the week, and stored none of them:

| Search | Posts that used the anti-trans framing |
|---|---|
| groomer | 0 of 47. Mostly pet grooming, abuse callouts and fandom; 2 pushed back against the slur |
| "women's sports" | 1 to 3 of 50. About 25 were fans; about 15 criticised the framing |
| "protect women" | 0 to 2 of 48. About half mocked the "protect women" crowd |
| "TRAs" | 0 of 50. Spanish and Portuguese "tras" |

On Bluesky these counts measure pushback and unrelated uses, not the rhetoric, so they are not tracked.

**On Twitter.** Once Twitter post counts were added, two searches suggested by the project owner were checked over 12 to 18 October 2026 (English, original posts a day):

| Search | Twitter original | Twitter reposts | Bluesky | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| troon OR troons | about 3,300 (2,886 to 4,654) | about 2,600 | about 10 | Beth's Anti-Transphobia Library, glossary: "Tranny / Troon", a slur for transgender people |
| "no such thing as trans" | about 100 (43 to 189) | about 10 | about 1 | The 74, 14 December 2023: an NYC education council member's "There is no such thing as trans kids" |
| "no such thing as a trans" | about 60 (32 to 109) | not counted | under 1 | as above |

Both are tracked on Twitter as the **anti-trans** group (the two phrasings as one search), with `x_only = true`, so Bluesky does not count them. On Twitter, as on Bluesky, a count includes people quoting the words to object. How large that share is on Twitter has not been checked: it needs reading a sample of posts (about $0.50 for 50 each), which the counts-only plan does not cover.

**The UK single-sex framing** ("single-sex spaces", "single-sex rights", "biology-based rights") is tracked on Twitter as its own **framing** group from 29 October 2026, at Emma's request, in two searches (the space phrasings; the rights phrasings). It is kept apart from the anti-trans group and from the coded-language score: these are also terms of UK equality law, so legal, policy and news discussion uses the same words, and no source we found calls the bare phrases dog whistles (docs/CODED-COMMUNITIES.md). The campaign phrases "protect single-sex spaces" and "defending sex-based rights", which are documented as dog whistles, stay in the coded-language list. Bluesky does not count them: its search ignores OR, and on 27 to 28 October 2026 it had about 2 posts a day for "single-sex spaces" and none for the others. Twitter cost: 8 count requests a day, about $0.04.
