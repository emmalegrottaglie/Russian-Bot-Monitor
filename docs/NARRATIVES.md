# Narrative keywords

Why the tracker has a "narratives" group, where each keyword comes from, and what it can and cannot show.

## Why

The right-wing group follows communities and searches named after parties and figures (r/Conservative, "MAGA", "AfD"). Published research on Russian state-linked influence operations describes something different. They push narratives (Ukraine aid as waste, Zelensky as illegitimate, culture-war topics) and attacks on named people, rarely party names. A group built on party names could miss most of what such operations post. The narratives group follows the talking points instead.

## What this group can and cannot show

- **It measures a topic, not an operation.** A search for "Zelensky" counts every post that uses the name: news, supporters, critics and debunkers. Researchers warn that keyword and hashtag volume cannot separate coordinated accounts from real users.
- **The topics move with the news.** Zelensky and Ukraine aid spike with events in the war, unrelated to bots. The strikes on Yandex were news themselves.
- **Documented reach was small.** ISD calls Operation Overload's influence underwhelming. Even if operations went quiet, their posts may be too few to move these counts.
- **Zelensky has extra weight.** Four of the eight searches are the same name in different languages, so the group median leans towards that one topic.

So this group gets the same tests as every other group: compared with the non-political control group, and with the same measure taken on random pre-strike dates. A drop after the strikes that also shows in the control group, or that random dates often produce, is not evidence. No change in this group would not show that bots are absent either.
