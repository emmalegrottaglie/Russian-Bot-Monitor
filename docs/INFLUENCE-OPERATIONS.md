# What a social media farm looks like: published reporting

Compiled 2 November 2026 at Emma's request, so readers can see what Russian "troll farms", "bot farms" and influence operations are, how they work, which topics they push, and how much effect they are measured to have. It feeds the "What a social media farm looks like" section on the site's About tab.

Rules, as elsewhere in this repository: each claim is what the named source found, not a fact we establish; "bot", "coordinated" and "Russian" are the source's attribution. Only public figures and organisations are named.

**How this was checked.** A research agent gathered the sources and read them on the original page. We then reread 9 of them on the original page or PDF ourselves (marked *rechecked*); all 9 matched what the agent reported. Sources seen only in a search summary are marked *unverified* and are not used on the site.

Much of the content was in Western European languages, not Romanian, so it seems aimed at wider audiences | Read in full |

**What the documents add up to.** Russian operations use LGBTQ+ people in two ways. They attack them as a symbol of Western decadence and of a threat to children and "traditional values" (EEAS 2023 and 2026, the Social Design Agency's plans, Storm-1516's smears of Zelensky, Doppelganger's anti-LGBTQ+ outlets). And, at least in 2016, they impersonated LGBTQ+ communities to deepen division (the Internet Research Agency's LGBT United). Anti-trans themes are named explicitly in the Social Design Agency's 2023 plan, Doppelganger's "My Pride" site and Storm-1516's "pro-transgender" fakes. The EUvsDisinfo cases show pro-Kremlin outlets repeating such stories, including about Britain. No source measures how much of the anti-trans or anti-LGBTQ+ content online comes from these operations.

## How much effect

| Source | What it found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Eady and colleagues, Nature Communications 14:62](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9829855/), 9 January 2023 | 1,496 US Twitter users linked to survey data in the 2016 election: 1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures to Internet Research Agency accounts, mostly strong Republicans; respondents saw about 25 times more posts from national news media than from Russian accounts; no meaningful relationship with changes in attitudes, polarisation or vote | Rechecked |
| Bail and colleagues, PNAS 117(1), 2019 ([PMC6955293](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6955293/)) | 1,239 US Twitter users in late 2017: no substantial effect of interacting with Internet Research Agency accounts on six measures of political attitudes and behaviour; contact concentrated among the already polarised | Agent-checked (abstract) |

Most of the operations above show large output and small measured reach. The largest numbers (such as the 34 million comments) are the operators' own claims.

## Also read (2 November 2026, documents fetched by Emma)

- **VIGINUM, Portal Kombat part two** ([copy](../library/2024-02-14_VIGINUM_Portal-Kombat-part-2.pdf), February 2024): a network of 193 "information portals" that produce no original content and relay Russian and pro-Russian accounts, news agencies and official sites, mainly about the war in Ukraine. Nothing on LGBTQ+ topics.
- **OpenAI, *Disrupting malicious uses of AI: June 2025***: "Helgoland Bite", accounts apparently from Russia using ChatGPT to write German-language content about the 2025 German election for a Telegram channel posing as local news (1,755 subscribers), which the Pravda network's German site republished. Nothing on LGBTQ+ topics.
- **US Department of Justice press release, 9 July 2024**, on the AI-enhanced bot farm (text supplied by Emma from justice.gov): RT developed the software and an FSB officer ran the farm; 968 Twitter accounts were searched and two domains (mlrtr.com, otanmail.com) seized. Its examples include accounts posing as residents of Minneapolis and of "Gresham" posting clips of President Putin in October and November 2023.
- **The Guardian, 2 April 2015** (text supplied by Emma): former staff at 55 Savushkina Street describe 12-hour shifts, rooms of about 20 people checked by editors, fines, daily "technical tasks" with set conclusions, ordinary posts mixed with political ones, and image banks of Western and Ukrainian leaders, many with racist or homophobic overtones.

## EUvsDisinfo cases about LGBTQ+ people, 2015 to 2022

Emma, 2 November 2026: catalogue the pro-Kremlin disinformation cases about LGBTQ+ people, by year, target country and theme, and compare the themes with the phrases this project counts.

**Data.** [Barbaro and Skumanich, *Euvsdisinfo dataset*, version 3](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/yhdtkszvgp/3) (Mendeley Data, 8 May 2023, doi:10.17632/yhdtkszvgp.3, CC BY 4.0): 14,497 cases from the EUvsDisinfo database (EEAS East StratCom Task Force), 6 January 2015 to 22 November 2022, each with its date, title, outlets, target countries and a summary of the claim. Downloaded 2 November 2026 (checksum matches the publisher's). It has no tags, so cases were found by words in the title and summary: LGBT, gay, homosexual, lesbian, transgender, queer, same-sex, sodomy, "gender ideology", "gender-neutral", sex change and similar. ## EUvsDisinfo cases about LGBTQ+ people, 2015 to 2026 (full database)

**Data.** The whole EUvsDisinfo database, downloaded on 2 November 2026 through its MCP server (`https://mcp.euvsd.info/mcp`, with an API key EUvsDisinfo gave Emma on request): 19,828 of the 19,832 cases it reported, from 2015 to 20 October 2026. The server could not return the other 4 (it fails on an empty field in each). Unlike the Mendeley copy, each case carries EUvsDisinfo's own keyword tags. A case counts if EUvsDisinfo tagged it `LGBTIQ+` or if its title or claim uses the words listed in the previous section. Of the 287 cases found, 167 were found both ways, 37 by the tag alone and 83 by words alone. Two near tags are not counted: of the 45 cases tagged `Perversion`, 27 neither carry `LGBTIQ+` nor name LGBTQ+ people, and most of those are about paedophilia or incest; `Gender issues` (9 cases) is about men and women. Scripts: `research/euvsdisinfo_mcp.py` (download, not in git) and `research/euvsdisinfo_lgbtq.py --mcp`. The cases (date, link, title, outlet web domains, countries, themes, matched words, tags) are in [euvsdisinfo-lgbtq-cases-mcp.csv](euvsdisinfo-lgbtq-cases-mcp.csv).

- **After 2022** (70 cases): the themes are the same as before. Imposed by the West 34, family and traditional values 33, Western decline 19, elections and politics 16, Ukraine 16, sport 15, children 14, transgender people 14. Sport appears mostly in 2024, the year of the Paris Olympics. France becomes a main target (24 cases, after 10 in 2015 to 2022). **The two copies disagree on 20 cases.** 185 of the Mendeley copy's 206 cases are also in the MCP download, and all 185 are counted there too. The other 20 (from 2016 to 2019, such as "The prosecution of gay men in Chechnya is fake news", June 2017, and two cases titled "Carl Bildt: Orthodoxy is the main enemy of the West") do not appear in the download, and the server's search does not find them either. EUvsDisinfo may have removed or merged them since 2022. One has been checked by hand: on 2 November 2026 the Chechnya case's page (euvsdisinfo.eu/report/the-prosecution-of-gay-men-in-chechnya-is-fake-news) returned "page not found", so that case at least was removed. The other 19 are unchecked; checking needs the case pages, which ask automated readers to prove they are not robots, so it is done in a browser by hand. Until they are checked, the count for 2015 to 2022 is given from each copy separately, not added together.

**What it cannot show.** The same as for the Mendeley copy: the database records what EUvsDisinfo's analysts chose to catalogue and which outlets published it, not reach or social media accounts. Matching by words also finds cases that mention LGBTQ+ people only in passing (the 21 cases found only by "gay", "pride" or "sodomy" are listed by the script for checking).
