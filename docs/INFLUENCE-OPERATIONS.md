# What a social media farm looks like: published reporting

Compiled 2 November 2026 at Emma's request, so readers can see what Russian "troll farms", "bot farms" and influence operations are, how they work, which topics they push, and how much effect they are measured to have. It feeds the "What a social media farm looks like" section on the site's About tab.

Rules, as elsewhere in this repository: each claim is what the named source found, not a fact we establish; "bot", "coordinated" and "Russian" are the source's attribution. Only public figures and organisations are named.

**How this was checked.** A research agent gathered the sources and read them on the original page. We then reread 9 of them on the original page or PDF ourselves (marked *rechecked*); all 9 matched what the agent reported. Sources seen only in a search summary are marked *unverified* and are not used on the site.

## Anti-LGBTQ topics

| Source | What it found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [EEAS, "FIMI targeting LGBTIQ+ people"](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/2023/EEAS-LGBTQ-Report-03-Digital%201.pdf), October 2023 | 31 cases of foreign information manipulation targeting LGBTIQ+ people from June 2022 to July 2023; over half attributed to Russia. Methods: impersonating media, forged documents, reused media. Narratives: children need protecting from LGBTIQ+ people, LGBTIQ+ people get preferential treatment in sport, and the overarching story that the West is in decline. The EEAS warns the cases are illustrative, not a measure of trends. Nothing specific to trans people | Rechecked |
| RFE/RL 2024 and ISD 2026 above | LGBT rights as harmful to children (Social Design Agency plans); LGBTQ+ people among Matryoshka's targets | Rechecked |
| Pamment and Tsurtsumia (Lund University, May 2025), pages 80 to 81 | **The rights of transgender people named in an operation's plan.** From May 2023 the Social Design Agency's campaign before the 2024 European Parliament elections aimed to discredit "liberal globalists" and help parties such as the AfD and National Rally in Germany, France, Spain, Italy and Poland. One of its four initial "thematic lines" (translated from Russian in the report) says abstract values are being imposed and children are "going crazy", and lists gender equality, the rights of transgender people and sexual minorities among the things "liberal globalists" would start wars for, against "true values" such as family. The report notes these lines appear in the initial proposal and were later changed and tied to measurable targets, such as raising the AfD's poll rating to 20% | Rechecked (full report) |
| [EUvsDisinfo database](https://euvsdisinfo.eu/disinformation-cases/?text=transgender) (EEAS East StratCom) | Catalogued cases of pro-Kremlin disinformation, each dated and attributed to an outlet. A search for "transgender" on 2 November 2026 returned two pages of cases from 2017 to 2025, including stories that calling a trans person by their sex at birth leads to jail in Scotland (April 2024), that Russia was unfairly accused over the Olympic women's boxing (August 2024) and that "transgenders are preparing a coup" in France (January 2025). The database also has "LGBTIQ+" and "Gender issues" tags. Not yet counted or read case by case; WebFetch is refused (403), the in-app browser works | Seen (search page) |

Doppelganger's fake LGBTQ+ outlet with "Trans youth" stories (DFRLab, 2024) and the agency's planning document above are the only direct evidence we have found of a Russian operation choosing anti-trans topics. The EUvsDisinfo cases show pro-Kremlin outlets repeating them, including about Britain.

## How much effect

| Source | What it found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Eady and colleagues, Nature Communications 14:62](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9829855/), 9 January 2023 | 1,496 US Twitter users linked to survey data in the 2016 election: 1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures to Internet Research Agency accounts, mostly strong Republicans; respondents saw about 25 times more posts from national news media than from Russian accounts; no meaningful relationship with changes in attitudes, polarisation or vote | Rechecked |
| Bail and colleagues, PNAS 117(1), 2019 ([PMC6955293](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6955293/)) | 1,239 US Twitter users in late 2017: no substantial effect of interacting with Internet Research Agency accounts on six measures of political attitudes and behaviour; contact concentrated among the already polarised | Agent-checked (abstract) |

Most of the operations above show large output and small measured reach. The largest numbers (such as the 34 million comments) are the operators' own claims.

## Yandex, infrastructure and the strikes

| Source | What it found | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Kyiv Independent, "No, Ukraine's strikes on Yandex data centers didn't cripple Russian influence ops"](https://kyivindependent.com/no-ukraines-strikes-on-yandex-data-centers-didnt-cripple-russian-influence-ops/), Martin Fornusek, 13 October 2026 | The Cloudflare chart behind the viral claim measures automated requests from Yandex's own network, mainly search crawlers. Poland's NASK saw no significant drop in disinformation accounts from 30 September to 11 October; Bot Blocker saw no dramatic drop and called Storm-1516 a commercial engagement-for-hire provider unlikely to run from Yandex data centres; Dave Troy notes troll operations run from abroad. Claims of a drop by Poland's digital minister, a Czech MEP and President Zelensky came without published data | Rechecked |
| [DomainTools, "Domain Registrars Powering Russian Disinformation"](https://dti.domaintools.com/domain-registrars-powering-russian-disinformation), 2025 | Operation websites use bulletproof hosting in Russia, Moldova and the Netherlands, Cloudflare to hide hosts, and registrars such as Namecheap and Reg.ru. No mention of Yandex | Agent-checked |

None of these sources shows an influence operation hosted on Yandex Cloud or Yandex data centres. Yandex appears as a search engine to game (RFE/RL, 2015).

## Topics for the narratives group

The topics these sources document (Ukraine and Western support for it, NATO, elections and party boosting, migration, cost of living, "globalists", the West as decadent, LGBTQ+ people and children, sport) overlap the narratives group already tracked (docs/NARRATIVES.md).
